Choose 17hats if

  • You want clients to pay by card or ACH inside the app, with saved cards, tipping and auto-pay.
  • You want bookkeeping in the same tool: categorized transactions, a profit and loss report and a sales tax report.
  • You bill retainers on a schedule and want recurring invoices charged automatically.
  • You take paid bookings, where the client pays when they schedule.
  • You run your business from your phone and want a mobile app.

Choose Thrive if

  • Your clients pay by bank transfer or through your own payment link, and your accountant handles the books.
  • You want time tracking, booking pages, workflows and client portals included, with no modules to add.
  • You want to offer packages in a proposal and let the client pick one.
  • You want open-source software you can self-host, or a workspace you can run with Claude or ChatGPT.
  • You or your clients work in a language other than English.

Feature by feature

Clients and pipeline

17hats keeps unlimited contacts and projects on every level, with tags, related contacts, custom fields and visual pipelines to move leads and clients through stages. Each project holds its documents, emails, phone logs and to-dos, and lead source reporting shows where your work comes from. Wedding pros get special handling for leads from The Knot and WeddingWire.

Thrive has a client list with pipeline stages, custom fields and a board view you can drag cards across. Each client record holds their projects, documents, invoices, emails and notes, and the Home page shows what's waiting on a client's response.

Both cover the basics well. 17hats has lead source reports; Thrive has boards for every list.

Proposals and contracts

17hats sends quotes, contracts and invoices, and can combine all three into one document so a lead can choose, sign and pay in one go. Choices the client makes on a quote carry over to the invoice. Clients can initial and sign contracts online. The number of documents per month depends on your level: 20, 35 or unlimited.

Thrive has eight proposal and contract starters, including a combined proposal and contract. A proposal can offer packages, say a €1,500 starter site, a €3,000 standard site and a €5,200 site with copywriting, and the client picks one before accepting. Clients sign by typing their name. There's no document limit.

17hats is stronger if you want quote, contract and payment in one step. Thrive is stronger for package choices and has no monthly cap.

Invoices and payments

17hats takes online payments through Stripe or Square, and US members can accept ACH through Stripe. Its site says 17hats doesn't add its own fee to card payments (some older plans do). Payment schedules, tipping and saved cards are available above the entry level, recurring billing is a $10/month module, and checks and cash can be recorded by hand.

Thrive doesn't process payments. Invoices show the payment instructions you write, such as bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment. There are no recurring invoices and no automatic payment reminders.

17hats is clearly stronger here.

Accounting

17hats includes bookkeeping: you categorize transactions and get profit and loss, sales tax, client sales and receivables reports. Bank connection costs $5/month and imports transactions as expenses, and a QuickBooks Online integration costs $5/month.

Thrive has no accounting: no expenses, no bank feeds, no tax reports. It shows invoice totals and what's overdue, and you keep using your accountant or bookkeeping app.

17hats wins if you want your books in the same place.

Time tracking

17hats offers time tracking as a $5/month module, for billable and non-billable time with hourly rates by project.

Thrive includes a timer and a time log. You pick unbilled entries from a project and turn them into an invoice draft, grouped by entry, day or description, and you review it before it goes out.

Thrive is the better fit for hourly work, and it's included in the price.

Scheduling

17hats online scheduling is a module: basic is $5/month, and advanced is $10/month and adds payment at booking and automatic Zoom links. It checks your calendar to prevent double bookings, supports group bookings and custom booking questions, and syncs with Google Calendar.

Thrive includes booking pages with your hours, notice, buffers and time zone, checked against your connected Google or Microsoft calendar. There's no payment at booking.

17hats is stronger for paid sessions; Thrive includes scheduling at no extra cost.

Email

17hats sends email from your own address inside a project, with templates, drafts and, above the entry level, incoming email sync, read receipts and scheduled sends. It also lists SMS texting as a new feature.

Thrive connects your Google or Microsoft mailbox, links conversations to clients and projects, and has a Needs reply view, drafts and templates. Nothing is sent without your review.

17hats does more, with scheduled sends, read receipts and texting.

Forms and automations

17hats has lead capture forms (1, 3 or 20 depending on level) with an auto-responder, and questionnaires with if/then logic. Workflows send documents and emails and add to-dos, and steps can run before, on or after a base date such as the project date. Advanced workflows sit on the top level.

Thrive forms have 1 to 50 questions, file uploads, nine starters and a website embed. Workflows react to events, such as a form being submitted or a proposal being accepted, with one trigger and up to 20 actions each. There are no date-based triggers.

17hats is stronger for timed sequences around an event date. Thrive includes forms and workflows on its one plan.

Client portal

The 17hats client portal is on the top level. Clients complete quotes, contracts, invoices and questionnaires, see event dates and locations, update their details and read your welcome message. You can set a password for each portal.

Thrive gives each client one private link, with no account to create, showing the proposals, contracts, invoices, forms and meetings you select, with anything waiting on them listed first.

Mobile, AI, languages and your data

17hats has a free mobile app.

Thrive runs in the browser only. It works with Claude and ChatGPT through MCP, the app is in 10 languages, and it's open source under the MIT license, so you can self-host it.

Pricing

As of November 2026, the 17hats pricing page lists $60 a month, $600 a year or $800 for two years, all in USD, with a 7-day free trial that you can restart. It also lists a 30-day money-back guarantee. The comparison grid on the same page describes three levels, Essentials, Standard and Premier, with different document limits, lead forms and support times; the client portal and advanced workflows are on Premier. Modules cost extra: basic scheduling $5/month, advanced scheduling $10/month, time tracking $5/month, recurring billing $10/month, bank connection $5/month and QuickBooks Online $5/month. There's also a free CRM with four invoices per quarter.

Thrive costs $10 a month or $100 a year, with everything included. The 7-day trial needs no card; connecting your email and calendar waits until you subscribe.

A worked example: a coach on 17hats' yearly plan who adds time tracking and basic scheduling pays $600 plus $120, so $720 a year at list price. On Thrive, the same coach pays $100.

Where Thrive falls short

  • No payment processing. No cards, ACH, saved cards, tipping or auto-pay. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record it.
  • No bookkeeping. No expenses, bank feeds, profit and loss or sales tax reports, and no QuickBooks integration.
  • No recurring invoices and no automatic payment reminders.
  • No payment at booking. Booking pages schedule; they don't take money.
  • No date-based workflow triggers. Workflows react to events, not "three days before the project date".
  • Typed-name signatures only, with no initials.
  • No scheduled emails, read receipts or texting.
  • No mobile app. Thrive runs in the browser.
  • No to-do lists or task boards. Thrive has Kanban boards for records, not a project task manager.

How to switch from 17hats to Thrive

Finish open projects and collect open invoices in 17hats, and start new work in Thrive.

  1. Export your contacts. In 17hats, open Contacts or Leads, click the gear icon at the top right and choose Export Contacts. You get a CSV file.
  2. Import them into Thrive. Go to Clients, then Import clients. Thrive matches your columns to its fields and shows a review step before saving, up to 500 rows per file.
  3. Rebuild your templates. Paste your quote and contract wording into Thrive's proposal and contract starters.
  4. Add your services and packages to the service catalog so proposals and invoices can reuse them.
  5. Write your payment instructions once in Settings → Invoices.
  6. Connect Google or Microsoft email and calendar, so emails land on client records and booking pages know when you're free.
  7. Embed your inquiry form and add a workflow or two, such as creating a contract draft when a proposal is accepted. See workflow ideas.

Questions

Is Thrive cheaper than 17hats?

Yes. As of November 2026, 17hats lists $600 a year, with modules extra. Thrive is $100 a year with everything included.

Can clients pay by card in Thrive?

Not inside Thrive. Your invoices show your payment instructions, which can include your own payment link, and you record the payment when it arrives.

Can I import my clients from 17hats?

Yes. Export contacts from 17hats as a CSV, then use Clients → Import clients in Thrive. You map the columns and review the rows before saving.

Does Thrive do bookkeeping like 17hats?

No. Thrive tracks invoices and recorded payments, but not expenses, bank transactions or taxes.

Is there a free trial?

Both have 7-day trials. Thrive's needs no card.
