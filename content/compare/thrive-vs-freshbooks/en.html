Choose FreshBooks if

  • You need real accounting: expenses, receipt scanning, bank import and reconciliation, double-entry reports and tax-time reports.
  • You want clients to pay invoices by card, bank transfer (ACH), Apple Pay or Google Pay inside the tool.
  • You bill retainers or recurring invoices and want late payment reminders sent for you.
  • Your accountant should log in and work from the same books.
  • You want a mobile app for invoices, receipts, mileage and time.

Choose Thrive if

  • Your hard part is the client side: proposals, contracts, onboarding, emails and calls, not bookkeeping.
  • You want contracts, package proposals, booking pages, website forms and client portals in one place.
  • Your clients mostly pay by bank transfer, and an accountant or separate tool already handles your books.
  • You want event-based workflows and an AI assistant (Claude or ChatGPT) connected to your workspace.
  • You want one plan at $10 a month, or open-source software you can host yourself.

These two tools overlap less than their feature lists suggest. FreshBooks is accounting software that also sends proposals. Thrive is a client workspace that also sends invoices. Thrive is not accounting software and doesn't try to be. Some freelancers are better served by FreshBooks alone; some use both.

Feature by feature

Accounting

This is FreshBooks' home ground. Its site describes "simple double-entry accounting": unlimited expenses, automatic receipt data capture, mobile mileage tracking, automated bank import and bank reconciliation, profit and loss, balance sheet and trial balance reports, a sales tax summary and accountant access. Several start on the Plus plan.

Thrive has none of this. There are no expenses, no bank feeds, no receipts and no tax reports.

FreshBooks is far stronger here. If you need bookkeeping in your business tool, this section decides it.

Invoices and payments

FreshBooks lets clients pay by credit and debit card, ACH bank transfer, Apple Pay, Google Pay and buy-now-pay-later services. It lists automated recurring invoices, automated late payment reminders, scheduled late fees, deposits, client credits and saved cards it can charge. Its pricing page lists processing at 2.9% + $0.30 per card transaction and 1% for ACH bank transfers.

Thrive invoices show the payment instructions you write once in Settings, such as your bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment. You set tax and due days, and can bill in several currencies. When a proposal with a billing schedule is accepted or signed, Thrive creates the deposit invoice. There are no recurring invoices and no automatic reminders.

FreshBooks is stronger here, by a wide margin.

Proposals and packages

FreshBooks offers unlimited estimates on every plan, and proposals from the Plus plan. Its proposal page describes custom templates, theme colors and fonts, clients who accept, ask questions and add an electronic signature, and proposals that convert to invoices.

Thrive has eight proposal and contract starters. A proposal can offer packages, and the client picks one before accepting: say a €2,400 starter brand kit, a €4,800 full identity or a €7,500 identity plus website. Their choice and the agreed price are recorded, and the deposit invoice can follow on its own.

Thrive is stronger for selling services with options.

Contracts and signatures

FreshBooks lists e-signatures, and its proposal page says clients can add their electronic signature to a proposal. We didn't find a separate contracts feature on its website, so we won't describe one.

Thrive has contracts as their own documents, or proposal and contract in one. Clients sign by typing their name, and Thrive keeps the name, consent, version and time. There are no drawn signatures and no third-party e-signature service. See proposals and contracts.

Client portal

FreshBooks has a client account portal. According to its help center, clients can view and comment on invoices, estimates and proposals, save card or bank details, pay several invoices at once, and share comments and files in a project's discussion area.

A Thrive portal is one private link per client, with no account to create. You choose exactly which proposals, contracts, invoices, forms, files and meetings appear. Clients sign, fill in forms and download documents there.

About even, with a different focus: FreshBooks centers on paying, Thrive on signing and onboarding.

Scheduling, email and forms

FreshBooks' pricing page lists customized email templates and automated client emails with dynamic fields. It doesn't list booking pages or a website form builder; it connects to other apps through its app marketplace, which includes Zapier.

Thrive connects your Google or Microsoft mailbox and calendar. Client emails link to client records, and you reply from reviewed drafts. Booking pages check your real calendar. Website forms can be embedded on your site and create the client and project when someone sends an inquiry.

Thrive is stronger here.

Automations

FreshBooks automates billing: recurring invoices, late payment reminders, late fees, and tracked time and expenses added to invoices.

Thrive has workflows for the rest of the client journey. Each one starts from one event, such as "proposal accepted" or "form submitted", and runs up to 20 actions: create the contract, draft an email, change the project status. There are no date-based triggers. Ideas in automating busy work.

Time tracking and projects

FreshBooks lists unlimited time tracking on every plan, with a Chrome timer extension and its mobile app. Logged hours can be pulled onto invoices, and you can see how a whole team spends its time. Premium adds project profitability.

Thrive has a timer and time entries by project, with hourly rates per workspace or project. Billable time becomes an invoice draft you review first. There are Kanban boards, but no task lists or team roles. More in time tracking for freelancers.

FreshBooks is stronger for teams and profitability; both handle a solo freelancer's billable hours well.

Mobile

FreshBooks has apps for iPhone, iPad and Android to send invoices, log expenses, snap receipts, track mileage and track time. Thrive runs in the browser only.

AI, languages and your data

Thrive is an MCP server: connect Claude or ChatGPT and ask it to find a client, draft a proposal or list overdue invoices. The app is in 10 languages, and the code is open source under the MIT license, so you can host it yourself. FreshBooks lets you export active and archived clients as a CSV file, according to its help center.

Pricing

As of November 2026, FreshBooks' pricing page lists four plans in US dollars:

  • Lite: $23 a month, or $248.40 a year billed yearly. Invoices to 5 clients, unlimited estimates, time tracking and expenses, tax-time reports.
  • Plus: $43 a month, or $464.40 a year. Up to 50 clients, plus proposals, retainers, accounting reports, bank reconciliation and accountant access.
  • Premium: $70 a month, or $756 a year. Unlimited clients, project profitability, custom email templates.
  • Select: custom pricing, with lower processing rates and 2 team members included.

Yearly billing is 10% off. Add-ons: team members at $11 a month each, Advanced Payments at $20 a month (included in Select), and payroll from $40 a month plus $6 per person. The client limit counts active and archived clients. There's a 30-day free trial with no card. Card and ACH processing fees are listed above.

Thrive costs $10 a month or $100 a year. One plan, everything included, no client limits. The 7-day trial needs no card; connecting email and calendar waits until you subscribe.

A worked example: a solo designer who wants proposals in FreshBooks needs Plus, which is $464.40 a year billed yearly. Thrive is $100 a year. But FreshBooks includes your books and card payments; Thrive includes contracts, booking pages, forms, email and workflows. If you need both sides, using both costs about $564 a year.

Where Thrive falls short

  • No accounting. No expenses, receipts, mileage, bank feeds, reconciliation, tax reports or accountant access.
  • No payment processing. Invoices show your payment instructions; clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment.
  • No recurring invoices, retainers or automatic payment reminders.
  • No payroll and no team members with their own access.
  • Typed-name signatures only.
  • No date-based workflow triggers.
  • No mobile app. Thrive runs in the browser.
  • No task lists beyond Kanban boards for records.

How to switch from FreshBooks to Thrive

Many people keep FreshBooks for accounting and move only the client side. Either way, finish open projects where they are and start new ones in Thrive.

  1. Export your clients from FreshBooks. Its help center says you can download active and archived clients as a CSV file from the clients section.
  2. Import them into Thrive. Go to Clients, then Import clients. Thrive matches your columns to its fields and shows a review step before anything is saved. Up to 500 rows per file.
  3. Rebuild your proposals and contracts from Thrive's starters, with your own wording.
  4. Add your services and packages to the service catalog, with fixed fees or hourly, daily, weekly or monthly rates.
  5. Write your payment instructions once in Settings, under Invoices.
  6. Connect your email and calendar (Google or Microsoft).
  7. Set up one or two workflows, such as "when a proposal is accepted, create the contract".

Questions

Is Thrive accounting software like FreshBooks?

No. Thrive has invoices and recorded payments, but no expenses, bank feeds, reconciliation or tax reports. If you need those, use FreshBooks or another accounting tool alongside Thrive.

Is Thrive cheaper than FreshBooks?

Yes. Thrive is $100 a year. FreshBooks starts at $248.40 a year billed yearly (Lite), and proposals start on Plus at $464.40 a year, as of November 2026. They do different jobs, though.

Can clients pay by card in Thrive?

Not inside Thrive. Invoices show the payment instructions you write, which can include your own payment link. FreshBooks processes card and ACH payments itself.

Can I use Thrive and FreshBooks together?

Yes. A common split is proposals, contracts, scheduling and client portals in Thrive, and invoicing with payments and bookkeeping in FreshBooks. There's no sync between them.

Can I import my clients from FreshBooks?

Yes. Export your clients from FreshBooks as CSV, then use Clients → Import clients in Thrive, with column matching and a review step.

Is there a free trial?

Thrive has a 7-day trial with no card. FreshBooks has a 30-day trial with no card.
