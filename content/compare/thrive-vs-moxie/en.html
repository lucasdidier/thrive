Choose Moxie if

  • You want clients to pay invoices by card, PayPal or ACH through Stripe or PayPal.
  • You bill retainers with recurring invoices and want reminders and late fees to run on their own.
  • You want expense tracking, a bank connection and a profit and loss report in the same app.
  • You want a business phone number for calls and texts, or a mobile app.

Choose Thrive if

  • Your clients pay by bank transfer or a payment link you already have.
  • You want workflows without moving to a higher plan.
  • You want proposals where the client picks a package and the deposit invoice follows on acceptance.
  • You want open-source software you could host yourself.
  • You want Claude or ChatGPT to work with your real clients and invoices.

Feature by feature

Everything about Moxie below comes from withmoxie.com and Moxie's help center, read in November 2026.

Clients and pipeline

Moxie has client records with custom fields, a client health snapshot of work done and payments received, and levels of access for collaborators. Its sales pipeline has stages you customize and move leads through.

Thrive has a client list with pipeline stages, custom fields, saved views and a Kanban board you drag cards across. Clients can be companies or individuals.

Both cover this well.

Proposals and contracts

Moxie offers proposal and contract templates where you save your packages and prices, with legal terms Moxie says were vetted by its attorney. Clients sign with an e-signature or a simple accept button. An onboarding flow can add welcome content, questionnaires and a meeting scheduler.

Thrive has eight proposal and contract starters. Pricing can include all services, let the client choose one package or several, or follow per-item rules with adjustable quantities, and the server checks the total. With project billing on, the deposit is invoiced the moment the client accepts. Contracts are signed with a typed name; Thrive records the name, consent, version and time.

Thrive's package choice is more detailed. Moxie's signing options and attorney-vetted terms go further.

Invoices and payments

Moxie connects Stripe or PayPal, so clients can pay by card, PayPal or ACH, in 81+ currencies. It has recurring invoices, automatic payment on the due date if you want it, automatic reminders, late fees, and reimbursable expenses you can add to an invoice.

Thrive invoices show the payment instructions you write, such as bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment. There are no recurring invoices and no automatic reminders, though overdue invoices appear in your daily or weekly summary email.

Moxie is clearly stronger for getting paid.

Scheduling and email

Moxie's scheduler has meeting types with their own durations, buffers and availability. It works with Google Calendar, Outlook, iCloud and CalDAV, adds Zoom or Google Meet links, sends email reminders, and can be embedded on your site. A universal calendar and inbox come with every plan.

Thrive connects one Google or Microsoft account. Client emails show up on each client, and you send from a draft you review. Booking pages have their own durations, buffers and notice, check your calendar, and add bookings matched to the client.

Moxie connects to more calendars and video tools.

Forms

Moxie forms can be embedded as a project inquiry form, saved as templates, paired with schedulers, proposals and contracts, and filled in without logging in.

Thrive forms take 1 to 50 questions, including file uploads, with nine starters. An email answer can create the client and a project-name answer a planned project. Embed inline or as a popup, in your fonts and colors.

Automations

Moxie's automations use a drag-and-drop builder and can react to events, like a form being filled in, or to time, like "after 7 days without a response". They come with the Pro plan and up.

Thrive workflows are on its only plan. Each starts from one event, such as a proposal accepted, a form submitted or a payment recorded, and runs up to 20 actions: create the client, create the contract, issue a deposit invoice, draft an email. There are no date-based triggers.

Moxie handles timed follow-ups; Thrive includes workflows for less.

Client portal

Moxie's portal lets clients schedule meetings, sign proposals, pay invoices and comment on projects, without shared passwords. You choose which files, projects and messages they see. On Pro, it is white-labeled with a personalized URL, plus ticketing for client requests.

A Thrive portal is one private link per client. Pending documents, forms and unpaid invoices come first. Clients can accept or sign, fill in forms, upload files and download PDFs. Nothing is shared until you select it.

Moxie's portal does more, including payment and tickets.

Projects, tasks and time

Moxie projects can hold tasks and subtasks, files and notes, with hourly, recurring or fixed billing. Tracked hours become an invoice in a few clicks.

Thrive projects hold documents, invoices, time and messages, with no task lists. Time has a timer and manual entries, and unbilled time becomes an invoice draft grouped by entry, day or description.

Moxie is stronger if you plan work in tasks.

Accounting

Moxie tracks income and expenses by client and category, attaches receipts, tracks vendors, connects a bank account or card through Plaid, integrates with QuickBooks on Pro, and produces a profit and loss report.

Thrive has no accounting. Use your accountant's tool alongside it.

AI, apps and your data

The Moxie Assistant, included in all plans, sets up projects, forms and emails from prompts, drafts replies, and writes email and business summaries. Moxie has apps on the App Store and Google Play, and Communicator adds a business number for calls and texts on Pro.

Thrive is an MCP server: connect Claude or ChatGPT and the assistant can read your clients, draft a proposal or preview an import, with nothing sent until you confirm. Thrive runs in the browser, in 10 languages, and its code is open source (MIT), so you can host it yourself.

Pricing

As of November 2026, Moxie lists three plans in US dollars:

  • Starter: $12 a month, or $10 a month billed annually ($120 a year).
  • Pro: $25 a month, or $20 a month billed annually ($240 a year). Adds workflow automations, white-label portal, Communicator and QuickBooks.
  • Teams: $40 a month, or $32 a month billed annually ($385 a year). Up to five team members.

The free trial lasts 14 days with no credit card. A migration specialist costs $149, once, on any paid plan.

Thrive has one plan: $10 a month or $100 a year, everything included. The trial is 7 days without a card; connecting email and calendar waits until you subscribe.

A freelancer who wants automations needs Moxie Pro: $240 a year. On Thrive, $100.

Where Thrive falls short

  • No payment processing. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment.
  • No recurring invoices, automatic payments or late fees.
  • No automatic payment reminders.
  • No date-based workflow triggers. One event trigger, up to 20 actions.
  • Typed-name signatures only.
  • No accounting: no expenses, bank feeds or tax reports.
  • No task lists; Kanban boards are for records.
  • No phone line, team seats or client ticketing.
  • No mobile app. Thrive runs in the browser.

How to switch from Moxie to Thrive

  1. Export your data. In Moxie, Settings → Data import / export, Export tab, downloads an Excel workbook (owner only). Save the clients sheet as a CSV file.
  2. Import it. In Thrive, Clients → Import clients: match columns, review rows, then save. Up to 500 rows per file.
  3. Add your services and packages to the service catalog.
  4. Rebuild proposal and contract templates from Thrive's starters with your own wording.
  5. Write payment instructions in Settings → Invoices.
  6. Connect Google or Microsoft email and calendar.
  7. Add a workflow or two, such as: when a form is submitted, create the client.
  8. Finish open projects in Moxie, and keep recurring invoices there until those clients know how to pay you next.

Questions

Is Thrive cheaper than Moxie?

Thrive is $100 a year. Moxie Starter is $120 a year billed annually, and Pro, which adds automations, is $240 (as of November 2026).

Can clients pay by card in Thrive?

Not inside Thrive. Invoices can include a payment link from a service you already use, and you record the payment.

Can I import my clients from Moxie?

Yes. Export your workspace from Moxie, save the clients sheet as CSV, then use Clients → Import clients in Thrive.

Does Thrive do bookkeeping?

No. There are no expenses, bank connections or tax reports.

Is there a free trial?

Yes. Thrive offers 7 days and Moxie 14 days, both without a credit card.
