Choose Bloom if

  • You're a photographer or videographer and want to deliver galleries with proofing, favorites and downloads from your CRM.
  • You want clients to pick a package, book a date, sign and pay a deposit in one booking form.
  • You want card, ACH, Venmo or PayPal payments, recurring invoices and auto-charge inside the app.
  • You want a portfolio website and a mobile app in the same subscription.
  • You want emails sent automatically before a project date.

Choose Thrive if

  • You bill by the hour or by the project and want tracked time to become invoice drafts.
  • Your clients pay by bank transfer or your own payment link, and you don't need galleries or a website builder.
  • You want your Google or Microsoft inbox linked to client records.
  • You want one plan with everything included, for $10 a month.
  • You want open-source software you can self-host, a workspace you can run with Claude or ChatGPT, or an app in your own language.

Feature by feature

Clients and pipeline

Bloom's CRM keeps each client's contact details, projects, notes, tasks and conversation history, with custom fields and tags. A lead is created when someone submits a lead form or sends a message, gets a status and tags, and can be turned into a project, contract and invoice in one click. Each project holds its contacts, invoices, payments, questionnaires, notes, files and events.

Thrive has a client list with pipeline stages, custom fields and a board view, and each client record holds their projects, documents, invoices, emails and notes. The Home page shows what's waiting on a client's response.

They're close here. Bloom's one-click lead conversion is handy; Thrive's boards cover every list.

Proposals, packages and booking

Bloom sends quotes with line items, and you can add a contract; when the client accepts, they're asked to pay. Its Instant Booking form lets a client choose a package and add-ons, pick a date, sign the contract and pay a deposit or the full amount in one flow, by link, popup or website embed. The Starter plan allows one booking form and one package.

Thrive proposals can offer packages, say a €1,500 starter site, a €3,000 standard site and a €5,200 site with copywriting, and the client picks one before accepting. Eight starters include a combined proposal and contract. There's no payment step: the client accepts, then gets an invoice with your payment instructions.

Bloom is stronger for session-style work where booking and payment happen together.

Contracts and signatures

Bloom offers legally binding e-signatures, contract templates from a pasted text or an imported PDF, project variables filled in for you, multiple signees and auto-countersign. Contracts can be attached to invoices, quotes and booking forms.

Thrive contracts are signed by typing a name, with consent, time and version recorded. There's no third-party e-signature service.

Bloom does more here.

Invoices and payments

Bloom takes card and ACH payments through Stripe or Square, plus PayPal, Venmo, Cash App and Zelle, and bank transfer details. It has payment schedules, tipping, card surcharges paid by the client, recurring invoices that can auto-charge a saved card, and a QuickBooks integration. Starter adds a 1.5% platform fee on card and ACH payments; Standard and Plus have none. Stripe's own fees still apply.

Thrive doesn't process payments. Invoices show the payment instructions you write, such as bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment. There are no recurring invoices and no automatic payment reminders.

Bloom is clearly stronger here.

Time tracking

Bloom's help center explains hourly billing as an invoice item where you enter the number of hours.

Thrive has a timer and a time log with hourly rates per project. You pick unbilled entries and turn them into an invoice draft, grouped by entry, day or description, and review it before it goes out.

Thrive is the better fit if you bill by the hour.

Scheduling and email

Bloom's calendar has two-way sync with Google, Apple and Outlook, buffers, blackout dates and Zoom meetings, and you can share a scheduler by link or embed. Projects keep conversation history, and its live messenger widget for your website lists two-way email sync.

Thrive includes booking pages with your hours, notice, buffers and time zone, checked against a connected Google or Microsoft calendar. It also connects that mailbox, links conversations to clients and projects, and has a Needs reply view, drafts and templates. Nothing is sent without your review.

Automations

Bloom automations send emails such as payment reminders, questionnaires and birthday messages, with project triggers, rules and date-based triggers. Separate workflows are task lists per service type, with subtasks and dates. Starter allows one of each.

Thrive workflows react to events, such as a form being submitted or a proposal being accepted, with one trigger and up to 20 actions: create a document draft or a deposit invoice, draft an email, change a project status and more. There are no date-based triggers and no task lists.

Bloom is stronger for reminders tied to dates; Thrive's workflows do more than send email.

Client portal and galleries

Bloom clients log in with their email and a password to see and pay invoices, sign contracts, download galleries and see project details. Galleries support proofing, favorites, download permissions, watermarks and activity tracking, with 50 GB, 500 GB or 1 TB of gallery storage by plan.

Thrive gives each client one private link, with no account to create, showing only the proposals, contracts, invoices, forms and meetings you select. Thrive has no image galleries.

For photographers, Bloom's galleries are a real reason to choose it.

Mobile, AI, languages and your data

Bloom has iOS and Android apps, and its website builder lists an AI writer. Its help center says you can enter information in any language and use Bloom anywhere, though card payments depend on where Stripe or Square operate.

Thrive runs in the browser only. It works with Claude and ChatGPT through MCP, so an assistant can find a client or draft a proposal for you. The app itself is in 10 languages, and Thrive is open source under the MIT license, so you can self-host it.

Pricing

As of November 2026, Bloom lists three plans: Starter at $14 a month billed yearly ($168 a year) or $18 billed monthly, Standard at $34 billed yearly ($408) or $44 monthly, and Plus at $66 billed yearly ($792) or $78 monthly. When we checked, the pricing page showed 50% off for the first 12 months: $7, $17 and $33 a month. Starter is limited to 3 active projects, one workflow, one automation, one booking form and one package, and has the 1.5% payment fee. Plus adds team members and removes "Powered by Bloom". Every plan has a 7-day free trial, extra storage is $10 a month per terabyte, and custom websites are $17 each.

Thrive costs $10 a month or $100 a year, with everything included. The 7-day trial needs no card; connecting your email and calendar waits until you subscribe.

To be fair: during Bloom's offer, Starter costs less than Thrive, within its limits. A worked example: a solo designer on Bloom Standard billed yearly pays $408 a year at list price, or $204 in the first year with the offer. On Thrive, the same designer pays $100.

Where Thrive falls short

  • No payment processing. No cards, ACH, payment apps, tipping or auto-charge. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record it.
  • No payment at booking and no instant booking form that combines package, date, contract and payment.
  • No image galleries for proofing and delivering photos.
  • No website or portfolio builder.
  • No recurring invoices and no automatic payment reminders.
  • No date-based workflow triggers. Workflows react to events.
  • Typed-name signatures only, with no countersigning or multiple signees.
  • No task lists. Thrive has Kanban boards for records, not project task management.
  • No mobile app and no QuickBooks integration or accounting.

How to switch from Bloom to Thrive

Finish open projects, galleries and payment plans in Bloom, and start new work in Thrive.

  1. Export your contacts. In Bloom, go to Settings → Exporting → Contact Data to get a CSV file.
  2. Import them into Thrive. Go to Clients, then Import clients. Thrive matches your columns to its fields and shows a review step before saving, up to 500 rows per file.
  3. Rebuild your templates. Paste your quote and contract wording into Thrive's proposal and contract starters.
  4. Add your services and packages to the service catalog.
  5. Write your payment instructions once in Settings → Invoices.
  6. Connect Google or Microsoft email and calendar, so emails land on client records and booking pages know when you're free.
  7. Embed your inquiry form and add a workflow or two. See workflow ideas.

Questions

Is Thrive cheaper than Bloom?

Than Standard and Plus, yes: Thrive is $100 a year, Bloom Standard is $408 a year billed yearly at list price. Bloom Starter is $168 a year, or less during its first-year offer, but limited to 3 active projects.

Can clients pay by card in Thrive?

Not inside Thrive. Your invoices show your payment instructions, which can include your own payment link, and you record the payment when it arrives.

Can I import my clients from Bloom?

Yes. Export your contacts from Bloom as a CSV, then use Clients → Import clients in Thrive, map the columns and review the rows.

Does Thrive have photo galleries?

No. If gallery delivery is central to your work, Bloom is the better choice.

Is there a free trial?

Both have 7-day trials. Thrive's needs no card.
