Choose Indy if

  • You want to start free: Indy's free plan covers 3 proposals, contracts and invoices a month.
  • You want clients to pay invoices online through Stripe or PayPal, with recurring invoices and payment reminders.
  • You manage your work with task lists and boards, and want clients to approve files with comments and versions.
  • You like chatting with clients in real time inside a project portal.

Choose Thrive if

  • You want clients to choose between packages in a proposal, with the deposit invoice created when they accept.
  • You want booking pages, client email and website forms linked to the same client records.
  • You want event-based workflows and an AI assistant (Claude or ChatGPT) working in your workspace.
  • You want one plan with everything at $10 a month, in one of 10 languages, or open-source software you can host yourself.

Indy and Thrive cover much of the same path, from first inquiry to paid invoice, and both are built for one person rather than a team. The differences are in payments, task management and how far each goes on scheduling, email and automation. Everything about Indy below comes from its own website and help center, as of November 2026.

Feature by feature

Clients and pipeline

Indy's People tool is a light CRM that groups each contact's invoices, files and documents, with contacts sorted as leads, clients or other. You can import contacts from a CSV file. On the free plan you can add up to 3 clients.

Thrive's client list has pipeline stages, a board view, custom fields and saved views. Clients can be companies or individuals, and there's no limit on how many you add.

Proposals and packages

Indy proposals use a drag-and-drop builder with six elements: text, image, embed, timeline, estimate and signature. You can set an expiry date, choose from over 50 fonts, and start from templates for design, video, SEO, marketing and more. An estimate can turn into an invoice automatically when the proposal is approved.

Thrive proposals start from eight starters and can offer packages. Say you offer a €2,400 starter brand kit, a €4,800 full identity and a €7,500 identity plus website: the client picks one, or optional extras, before accepting. Their choice and price are recorded, and with a billing schedule the deposit invoice is created on acceptance. See offering packages in proposals.

Thrive is stronger for packages and options; Indy gives you more freedom in proposal layout.

Contracts and signatures

Indy offers lawyer-made contract templates for designers, developers, photographers, copywriters and more, plus a consulting agreement, an NDA and a subcontractor agreement. You can sign with a signature generated from text or an uploaded image, send to several parties at once, and clients sign online without an Indy account.

Thrive has contract starters and a combined proposal plus contract. Clients sign by typing their name, without an account, and Thrive records the name, consent, version and time. There are no drawn or uploaded signatures.

Indy is stronger here, mainly for its template library and signing options.

Invoices and payments

Indy invoices can be paid online through Stripe (credit, debit, bank transfer) or PayPal, and the invoice updates when paid. Indy says it charges no processing fees of its own; Stripe or PayPal charge theirs. Invoices support taxes, discounts, late fees, deposits, your choice of currency and tracked time as line items. Its invoices page mentions automated payment reminders, and recurring invoice series are part of Pro.

Thrive invoices show the payment instructions you write once in Settings, such as bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment. There are no recurring invoices and no automatic reminders.

Indy is stronger here.

Scheduling and email

Indy's Calendar shows events, tasks and due items by day, week or month. You can invite guests, add Google Meet links and, on Pro, sync with Google Calendar. Pro also lists a Gmail integration.

Thrive connects your Google or Microsoft mailbox and calendar. Client emails link to client records, and you reply from reviewed drafts. Booking pages let clients pick a time that your real calendar says is free. Read booking client calls.

Thrive is stronger for client booking and email linked to clients.

Forms

Indy forms come from templates, can be shared as a link on every plan and embedded on your site with Pro. New responses can become contacts with a click.

Thrive forms start from nine starters, with up to 50 questions and file uploads. You can embed them on your site, and an inquiry form can create the client and a planned project on its own. See generating leads with forms.

Automations

Indy's help center describes automations you add to an invoice: you pick an event and set an action. Pro mentions document automations and a Zapier integration.

Thrive workflows start from one event, such as "proposal accepted" or "form submitted", and run up to 20 actions: create the contract, draft an email, change the project status. They're included in the one plan. There are no date-based triggers. Ideas in automating busy work.

Client portal and projects

Indy's project portal is a shared space with real-time chat, file sharing and visible tasks, with privacy toggles for what clients see. Files can have an approver, comments and new versions. Tasks have list and board views, due dates and tags. Indy lists project portals and the branded client portal as Pro features.

A Thrive portal is one private link per client with the proposals, contracts, invoices, forms, files and meetings you choose. Clients sign and fill in forms there, but there's no chat. Projects have Kanban boards, but no task lists or file approvals. See client portals.

Indy is stronger for day-to-day project collaboration.

Time tracking

Indy has a start-and-pause timer or manual entries, time attached to projects, filters by invoice status, and time added to invoices as line items.

Thrive has a timer and time entries by project with hourly rates. Billable time becomes an invoice draft you review before sending. More in time tracking for freelancers. Both cover this well.

AI, languages and your data

Indy Pro includes an AI digital assistant for writing contracts and proposals. Thrive is an MCP server instead: connect Claude or ChatGPT and it works with your real clients, proposals and invoices, while sending always waits for your approval. Thrive's app is in 10 languages, and its code is open source under the MIT license, so you can host it yourself.

Pricing

As of November 2026, Indy's pricing page lists two plans:

  • Free: $0, no credit card. Projects, calendar, forms, tasks and time tracker, plus 3 proposals, contracts and invoices a month, up to 3 clients, and 10GB of storage.
  • Pro: $25 a month billed monthly, $18.75 a month billed $225 yearly, or $12.50 a month billed $300 every 2 years. Unlimited documents and clients, AI assistant, branded client portal, recurring invoices, Zapier, Google Calendar sync, embedded forms, white labeling, Gmail integration and 1TB of storage.

Pro has a 7-day free trial, and Indy says paid subscriptions are non-refundable. Stripe and PayPal charge their own processing fees.

Thrive costs $10 a month or $100 a year. One plan, everything included. The 7-day trial needs no card; connecting email and calendar waits until you subscribe.

A worked example: a solo designer who sends more than 3 proposals a month needs Indy Pro, which is $225 a year, or $150 a year on the 2-year plan ($300 upfront). Thrive is $100 a year. If you send only a few documents a month, Indy's free plan costs nothing.

Where Thrive falls short

  • No free plan. Thrive has a 7-day trial, then $10 a month.
  • No payment processing. Invoices show your payment instructions; clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment.
  • No recurring invoices and no automatic payment reminders.
  • Typed-name signatures only. No uploaded signatures.
  • No task lists, file approvals or client chat. Kanban boards cover records only.
  • No date-based workflow triggers. Workflows react to events.
  • No Zapier integration and no mobile app; Thrive runs in the browser.

How to switch from Indy to Thrive

Finish open projects in Indy and start new ones in Thrive.

  1. Export your clients from Indy as a spreadsheet, then save it as CSV.
  2. Import them into Thrive. Go to Clients, then Import clients. Thrive matches your columns to its fields and shows a review step before anything is saved. Up to 500 rows per file.
  3. Rebuild your proposals and contracts from Thrive's starters, with your own wording.
  4. Add your services and packages to the service catalog.
  5. Write your payment instructions once in Settings, under Invoices. Your own Stripe or PayPal payment link works here.
  6. Connect your email and calendar (Google or Microsoft) and publish a booking page.
  7. Set up one or two workflows, such as "when a proposal is accepted, create the contract".

Questions

Is Thrive cheaper than Indy?

Than Indy Pro, yes: Thrive is $100 a year, Indy Pro is $225 a year or $300 for 2 years, as of November 2026. Indy's free plan costs nothing if 3 documents a month is enough.

Can clients pay by card in Thrive?

Not inside Thrive. Invoices show the payment instructions you write, which can include your own payment link. Indy connects to Stripe and PayPal.

Can I import my clients from Indy?

Yes. Save your client list as a CSV file, then use Clients → Import clients in Thrive, with column matching and a review step.

Does Thrive have task management like Indy?

No. Thrive has Kanban boards for clients, projects and other records, but no task lists.

Is there a free trial?

Thrive has a 7-day trial with no card. Indy has a free plan and a 7-day trial of Pro.
