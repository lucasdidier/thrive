Admin rarely goes wrong all at once. An invoice slips past its due date, a few hours never get billed, and a proposal sits unanswered for two weeks because you forgot to ask. None of it is hard to fix. It just needs a regular moment when you look. Thirty minutes once a week is usually enough to keep your business healthy and to start Monday knowing where you stand.

Why a fixed weekly slot works

When admin has no set time, it happens when something breaks: a client asks about an invoice you never sent, or you notice in March that January's hours were never billed. A fixed slot changes that. Problems show up while they are still small, and you stop carrying a vague worry that something is slipping.

Friday afternoon works for many freelancers. Client work winds down, the week is fresh in your mind, and anything you send still reaches people before the weekend. Monday morning works too. What matters is that it's the same slot every week, in your calendar, and short enough that you never skip it.

Keep the order the same each time. Money first, because it matters most and gets harder to collect the longer you wait. Then the people waiting on you, then next week.

The routine at a glance

Friday, 30 minutes
1. Money in (8 min): overdue and outstanding invoices, reminders, payments received.
2. Unbilled time (5 min): fill gaps in this week's log, invoice what is due.
3. Waiting on clients (4 min): proposals and contracts with no answer.
4. Waiting on you (6 min): new inquiries and emails that need a reply.
5. Next week (5 min): meetings, project due dates, the one thing at risk.
6. Numbers (2 min): billable hours, paid this month, open pipeline.

The times are a guide. Some weeks step one takes two minutes, some weeks it takes fifteen. If the whole thing regularly runs over an hour, that's a sign you are doing project work in your admin slot, or that something upstream needs fixing, like payment terms.

1. Check money in (8 minutes)

Start with every invoice that isn't fully paid. Sort them into three groups:

  • Overdue: past the due date. These get a reminder today.
  • Due soon: due in the next week. Nothing to do yet, but note them.
  • Paid but not recorded: check your bank and mark anything that arrived, so your list stays true.

For overdue invoices, follow a calm rhythm: a friendly reminder a few days late, a second one around ten days late asking if anything is blocking payment, then a call. Keep each message short, with the invoice number, due date, amount and a link. Our guide on how to invoice clients has a reminder template you can copy.

2. Bill unbilled time (5 minutes)

If you work by the hour, or bill extra time on top of fixed fees, Friday is a good moment to catch up. First fill any gaps in this week's log while you still remember Tuesday. Then decide what to bill. Some clients get a weekly invoice, most get monthly, and small amounts can wait until the end of the project. The point is to look, so hours don't quietly pile up and age.

A worked example: you logged 6.5 extra hours at €90 for a client this month. That's €585. Left alone for three months, it becomes an awkward €1,755 surprise for them and a cash gap for you.

3. Nudge documents awaiting a response (4 minutes)

A proposal that nobody has answered is not a "no". Usually it's a busy client, a forwarded email, or a decision waiting on someone else. List every proposal and contract you sent that is still open, and for anything older than about a week, send a short note:

Hi Sam, just checking the proposal for the website refresh reached you. Happy to answer any questions or adjust the scope. Would a 15-minute call next week help?

Two follow-ups is a sensible limit. After that, mark it as lost in your own notes and move on.

4. Answer leads and emails (6 minutes)

New inquiries go cold fast. Someone who filled in your contact form on Monday and hears back on Friday may already have hired someone else. Your weekly check is a safety net, not the main response time. Aim to answer leads within a day, and use Friday to make sure nothing fell through.

Look at two places: new inquiries from your website or forms, and client emails still waiting for your reply. For each, either answer now or block a time to do it. 5. Look at next week (5 minutes)

Open your calendar and your project list side by side. You're looking for collisions and surprises:

  • Meetings that need preparation, such as a presentation or a review.
  • Projects with a due date next week, or ones already late.
  • Days that are already full, so you don't promise new work on them.

Write down the one project most at risk and the first thing you'll do on it on Monday. If you're balancing several clients at once, keep this light. The goal here is just to see the week coming.

6. A few numbers worth watching (2 minutes)

You don't need a full dashboard. Three numbers, checked every week, tell you most of what you need:

  • Billable hours this week or month. If you aim for 25 billable hours a week and keep landing at 15, your rates or your admin load need a look.
  • Paid this month. Money that actually arrived, not money invoiced. Compare it with your monthly costs and what you pay yourself.
  • Open pipeline. How many proposals are out and what they're worth. If it's empty in week three of the month, next month will be quiet, so spend some time on outreach now.

Write them in a simple note each Friday. After two months you'll see trends that no single week shows.
