Moxie is a business app made for freelancers, and it shows. Its website talks to solopreneurs, designers, developers, coaches and virtual assistants, and its plans start low. If you are comparing it with other tools, this article gives you a fair picture of Moxie and Thrive side by side. Everything said about Moxie comes from its own website, withmoxie.com, as of November 2026. If you knew the app as Hectic: Moxie's site still uses that name for its mobile app.

Who Moxie is great for

Moxie covers most of what a freelancer needs to sell, deliver and get paid, and adds a few things many tools don't. From its website:

  • Proposals and contracts from templates, with legal terms Moxie says were vetted by its attorney, and "e-signatures or simple accept buttons".
  • Invoicing with online payments through Stripe and PayPal, automatic recurring invoices and automatic reminders.
  • A meeting scheduler with unlimited meeting types, Zoom and Google Meet integrations, and payment at booking.
  • Embeddable forms with conditional paths and file uploads.
  • Time tracking linked to clients, projects and invoices.
  • A client portal, where you choose what clients can see.
  • Basic accounting, with integrations including Plaid and QuickBooks.
  • Communicator, a work inbox plus a business phone line, available in the US, Canada and the UK.
  • Mobile apps on the App Store and Google Play.

If you want clients to pay by card from the invoice, retainers billed every month without you lifting a finger, reminders that chase late payers for you, or a business phone number, Moxie has those and Thrive doesn't.

Moxie pricing as of November 2026

Moxie offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card required. Its pricing page lists three plans in US dollars:

  • Starter: $12 a month, or $10 a month billed annually ($120 a year). Client and project management, invoicing and payments, proposals, time tracking, a sales pipeline, basic accounting, forms, scheduling, a shared calendar and inbox, and the Moxie AI assistant.
  • Pro: $25 a month, or $20 a month billed annually ($240 a year). Adds a custom domain, a white-labeled client portal, workflow automations, a Communicator phone line, API and automation integrations, QuickBooks and unlimited project collaborators.
  • Teams: $40 a month, or $32 a month billed annually ($385 a year). Adds up to five team members and a second Communicator line.

Moxie also offers a paid migration service: for $149, one time, a specialist moves your data from a CSV export of your previous app. It is available to anyone on a paid plan.

Where Thrive is different

Thrive covers the same core path, from first inquiry to paid invoice: clients, projects, proposals, contracts, invoices, time tracking, email linked to clients, booking pages, website forms, workflows and client portals. The differences are in how it is built and what it leaves out.

Open source, and yours to host

Thrive's code is public under the MIT licence. You can use Thrive Cloud for $10 a month or $100 a year, with a 7-day trial and no card. Or you can run your own copy on your own Cloudflare and Supabase accounts, free. If the company behind a tool ever changes direction, your software doesn't have to. We go into what that means in open-source software for your freelance business.

One plan with workflows included

Thrive has a single plan, and workflows are part of it. A workflow starts from one event and runs one or more actions: when a proposal is accepted, create the contract; when an inquiry form is submitted, create the client. Moxie lists workflow automations from its Pro plan. See automating busy work for ideas.

Works with the AI you already use

Moxie includes its own AI assistant. Thrive takes another route: it is an MCP server, so you connect Claude or ChatGPT and the assistant works with your real clients, invoices and projects. It can draft a proposal from your call notes, list overdue invoices with a reminder draft for each, or preview a client import. Sending and issuing always wait for your confirmation. Read how to use AI to run your admin.

Client portals and package proposals

A Thrive client portal is one private link per client with their proposals, contracts, invoices, forms and files. A proposal can offer packages (for example a €2,400, €4,800 or €7,200 website) and the client picks one before accepting. Our guide to client portals shows how to set one up.

Ten languages

Thrive's interface is available in ten languages, which matters if you or your clients don't work in English.

What Thrive doesn't do

Moxie does several things Thrive doesn't. Plainly:

  • No payment processing. Thrive invoices show the payment instructions you write, such as bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive, and you record the payment.
  • No recurring invoices.
  • No automatic payment reminders and no date-based workflow triggers. You chase late invoices yourself, or ask a connected AI assistant to draft the reminders.
  • Typed-name signatures only.
  • No accounting, no phone line, no team seats and no task lists.
  • No mobile apps. Thrive runs in the browser.

Who should pick which

Pick Moxie if you want card or PayPal payments on your invoices, recurring billing, reminders that run on their own, basic bookkeeping in the same app, a business phone line, or a mobile app for work on the go.

Pick Thrive if your clients already pay by bank transfer or a payment link you have, you want open-source software you could host yourself, you want workflows without moving up a plan, or you want Claude or ChatGPT to help with your admin.

How to switch

Pick a quiet week and give it an afternoon.

  1. Put your client list in a CSV file. Only an email column is required; name, company, phone and address are useful.
  2. Import it. In Thrive, open Clients and choose Import clients. Match the columns to Thrive's fields, then review. A file can hold up to 500 rows. Duplicate emails are skipped, and rows with problems are flagged before anything is saved. A connected AI assistant can also preview the import for you.
  3. Rebuild your templates. Add your services and prices, then start proposals and contracts from Thrive's starters with your own wording. Save your logo and colors once.
  4. Write your payment instructions, such as bank details or a payment link, so every invoice shows them.
  5. Connect email and calendar. Connect your Google or Microsoft account so client emails appear on each client and your booking pages check your calendar. This comes with the paid plan, after the trial.
  6. Let open work finish in Moxie. Keep any active recurring invoices running there until you have told those clients how to pay going forward, and start new projects in Thrive.
