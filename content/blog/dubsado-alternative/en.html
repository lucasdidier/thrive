Dubsado has a loyal following among photographers, designers, coaches and virtual assistants, and it earns it with deep forms, workflows and payment options. But depth comes with setup time and a price that grows with the plan you need. If you're wondering whether something simpler would do, here is a fair comparison of Dubsado and Thrive, including where Dubsado is clearly the better choice.

What Dubsado is great at

Dubsado describes itself as built for independent service providers and small teams. On its website it lists photographers, designers, copywriters, coaches, virtual assistants, marketers, bookkeepers, event planners and florists among its users. As of November 2026, its official site says it offers:

  • Payments through Dubsado Payments, powered by Stripe, with cards, ACH bank transfers, Klarna, Affirm and Tap to Pay. PayPal and Square can also be connected. Dubsado Payments is available in 38 countries, according to its help center.
  • Payment plans and automated reminders, so deposits and instalments are chased for you.
  • Contracts with legally binding e-signatures, which can be sent together with a proposal and invoice.
  • Workflows that run multi-step automations when a client submits a form, signs a contract or pays.
  • A branded client portal, lead capture forms you can embed on your website, a scheduler with two-way calendar sync, and a mobile app for iOS and Android.

If you want card payments, payment plans with automatic reminders and long multi-step automations, Dubsado covers all of that, and Thrive doesn't do the payment part at all.

Dubsado pricing (as of November 2026)

Dubsado lists two plans. Starter is $35/month or $335/year. Premier is $55/month or $525/year. The scheduler, automated workflows and unlimited lead forms are on Premier; Starter includes one lead capture form. Extra users and brands are paid add-ons. The free trial lasts 21 days with full Premier features and no credit card.

Where Thrive is different

Thrive is a workspace for freelancers and small studios that covers the same day-to-day path, from inquiry to paid invoice, with less to configure.

One plan, everything included

Thrive costs $10 a month or $100 a year. Workflows, booking pages, website forms and client portals are part of the one plan, not a higher tier. The 7-day trial needs no card. Connecting email and calendar waits until you subscribe.

Open source

Thrive's code is published under the MIT license. Use the hosted version, or self-host it if you want your client records on your own server. Your data stays exportable either way.

Simple workflows

Thrive workflows follow one pattern: when something happens, do something. "When a proposal is accepted, create the contract." "When the contract is signed, create the project." They're quick to set up and easy to read later. The trade-off is that they're shorter than Dubsado's multi-step workflows, and they react to events, not dates.

Proposals where the client picks a package

A Thrive proposal can show packages side by side, for example a €1,800 half-day shoot, a €3,200 full day and a €4,600 full day with editing. The client picks one and accepts online. If you use project billing, the deposit invoice is created automatically when they accept or sign. Our guide to pricing your freelance work explains when packages help.

Time tracking built in

Thrive has a timer and time entries by project, and billable hours become invoice drafts. Useful if some of your work is hourly. See time tracking for freelancers.

AI assistants and 10 languages

You can connect Claude or ChatGPT to Thrive through MCP and ask it to look things up or prepare drafts, with emails sent only after you approve them. The app is available in 10 languages, including German, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Dutch.

Where Thrive is more limited

  • No payment processing. Your invoices show the payment instructions you write, like bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment.
  • Typed-name signatures only, with no third-party e-signature service.
  • No recurring invoices and no automatic payment reminders.
  • No date-based workflow triggers, such as "two days before the session".
  • No mobile app. Thrive runs in the browser.

Who should pick which

Pick Dubsado if you take card payments and payment plans, want reminders to go out on their own, or rely on long automations with several steps and delays. Wedding photographers and planners with many instalments per client are a good example.

Pick Thrive if your clients pay by bank transfer, you want to be set up in an afternoon rather than a week, or you want open-source software at $10 a month. It fits designers, developers, consultants and small studios who send a handful of proposals a month and want clients, documents, invoices, time and email in one place.

A quick test: list the automations you actually use in Dubsado today. If most are "when X happens, send or create Y", Thrive's workflows will cover them. If you depend on reminders timed to dates, stay where you are or plan to send those yourself.

How to switch from Dubsado to Thrive

Let current projects finish in Dubsado and start new ones in Thrive. That way no client gets two sets of links mid-project.

  1. Export your clients to a spreadsheet and save it as CSV.
  2. Import them under Clients, then Import clients. Thrive matches your columns to its fields and lets you review and fix rows before saving. Up to 500 rows per file.
  3. Rebuild your forms and templates. Recreate your inquiry form from a Thrive form template and embed it on your website. Paste your proposal and contract wording into Thrive's starters, and add your services and packages.
  4. Add your payment instructions in Settings, under Invoices.
  5. Connect Gmail or Outlook and your calendar, so client emails link to their records and booking pages show your real availability.
  6. Recreate your key workflows one at a time, starting with "proposal accepted, create contract".
