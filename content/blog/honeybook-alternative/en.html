HoneyBook is one of the best-known tools for running a small service business, and for good reason. But it isn't the right fit for everyone. Maybe you work outside the countries it supports, maybe you want something cheaper and simpler, or maybe you'd like software you can host yourself. This is a fair look at where HoneyBook shines, where Thrive is different, and how to decide between them.

What HoneyBook is great at

HoneyBook calls itself a CRM for small businesses, "from creatives to consultants". On its own website it highlights event services, marketing, photo and video, and professional services. Here is what it offers, as of November 2026, according to its official site:

  • Payments built in. Clients can pay by card, bank transfer (ACH), Apple Pay or Google Pay, and HoneyBook processes the payment. It sends automated reminders about upcoming or overdue payments, and clients can turn on autopay for recurring invoices or retainers.
  • Contracts with typed or drawn signatures. Clients sign with a typed or hand-drawn signature, and you can collect a deposit right after they sign.
  • A mobile app for iOS and Android.
  • Automations, scheduling and a client portal, plus HoneyBook AI on every plan.

If you need clients to pay you by card inside the same tool that sent the contract, HoneyBook does that well, and Thrive does not. That's the single biggest difference, so it's worth saying first.

HoneyBook pricing (as of November 2026)

HoneyBook's pricing page lists three plans, billed yearly: Starter at $29/month, Essentials at $49/month and Premium at $109/month. Billing monthly costs more. Automations and the full scheduler start on Essentials; Starter includes one session type for scheduling and up to 2 lead forms. There's a 30-day free trial with no credit card. Card processing has its own fees, listed on HoneyBook's payments page.

One practical point: HoneyBook says it is currently available for business owners in the U.S., Canada, the UK and Australia. If you freelance from Portugal, Germany or Brazil, that alone may settle the question.

Where Thrive is different

Thrive is a workspace for freelancers and small studios: clients, projects, proposals, contracts, invoices, time tracking, email, scheduling, forms, workflows and client portals in one place. A few things set it apart.

Open source and self-hostable

Thrive's code is public under the MIT license. You can use the hosted version at runonthrive.com, or run it on your own server if you'd rather keep your client data under your own control. If Thrive ever stopped being a fit, the code would still be there.

One price, one plan

Thrive costs $10 a month or $100 a year. Everything is included: workflows, booking pages, forms, client portals and the AI connection are not split across tiers. The trial lasts 7 days and doesn't ask for a card. One note: connecting your email and calendar waits until you subscribe.

Proposals with packages

A Thrive proposal can offer packages, and the client picks one before accepting. Say you offer a €2,400 starter brand kit, a €4,800 full identity and a €7,500 identity plus website. The client sees all three, picks the one that fits, and accepts online. Their choice and the agreed price are recorded.

Time tracking that becomes invoices

Thrive has a built-in timer and time entries by project. Billable time turns into invoice drafts, so hourly work doesn't need a second app. More on that in time tracking for freelancers.

Works with your AI assistant

Thrive is an MCP server, which means you can connect Claude or ChatGPT to your workspace and ask it to find a client, draft a proposal or prepare an invoice. Anything that sends an email waits for your approval. See how to use AI to run the admin side of your business.

10 languages

The app is available in English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese (Brazil), Polish, Turkish and Indonesian.

Where Thrive is more limited

Being clear about this saves you a wasted trial:

  • No payment processing. Thrive invoices show the payment instructions you write, such as your bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive, and you record the payment.
  • Typed-name signatures only. No drawn signatures and no third-party e-signature service.
  • No recurring invoices and no automatic payment reminders. You send reminders yourself.
  • No date-based workflow triggers. Workflows react to events, like "proposal accepted" or "form submitted", not to "three days before the due date".
  • Browser only. Thrive runs in your browser; HoneyBook has a mobile app.

Who should pick which

Pick HoneyBook if you're based in the U.S., Canada, the UK or Australia, and you want clients to pay by card or ACH inside the tool, with autopay, automatic reminders and a mobile app. Photographers and event businesses that take lots of deposits on the go are a natural fit.

Pick Thrive if your clients mostly pay by bank transfer anyway, you work outside HoneyBook's countries, you bill partly by the hour, or you want open-source software at $10 a month. It suits designers, developers, consultants and small studios who want proposals, contracts, invoices and time in one calm place, and who like working with an AI assistant.

If you're still unsure, write down how your last five clients paid you. If most paid by card through a link in your current tool, keep a payment processor. If most paid by bank transfer, payment processing isn't what you're really paying for.

How to switch from HoneyBook to Thrive

You don't have to move everything at once. Finish open projects where they are, and start new ones in Thrive.

  1. Export your clients from HoneyBook as a spreadsheet, then save it as CSV.
  2. Import them into Thrive. Go to Clients, then Import clients. Thrive gives you an example file, matches your columns to its fields, and shows a review step where you can fix rows before anything is saved. Up to 500 rows per file.
  3. Rebuild your templates. Start from one of Thrive's proposal or contract starters, paste in your own wording, and add your services and packages to the service catalog.
  4. Write your payment instructions once in Settings, under Invoices. They appear on every invoice.
  5. Connect your email and calendar (Google or Microsoft) so client emails link to client records and your booking pages know when you're busy.
  6. Set up one or two workflows, such as "when a proposal is accepted, create the contract". Our list of workflow ideas has more.
