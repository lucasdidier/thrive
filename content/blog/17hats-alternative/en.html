17hats has been helping solopreneurs run their business for years, and it packs a lot into one account: quotes, contracts, invoices, payments, bookkeeping and scheduling. If you're reading this, you're probably wondering whether you need all of it, or whether a lighter, cheaper tool would suit you better. Here is a fair comparison of 17hats and Thrive, including the cases where 17hats is the better pick.

What 17hats is great at

17hats says it serves more than 100 industries, from balloon artists to photographers, coaches and designers, and is aimed at solopreneurs, freelancers and small business owners. As of November 2026, its official website lists:

  • One document for quote, contract and invoice. You can combine all three, so the client reviews, signs and pays in one go. Clients can initial and sign contracts online.
  • Online payments through Stripe or Square, with payment schedules, tipping, saved cards and auto-pay. Checks and cash can be recorded by hand.
  • Built-in bookkeeping to see whether you're profitable without a separate app, with bank connection as an add-on.
  • Online scheduling, with payment at booking and Zoom links on the advanced scheduling option.
  • Workflows, lead capture forms, a client portal and a free mobile app for Apple and Android.

If you want to take card payments, keep simple books and book paid sessions in the same place, 17hats covers all three. Thrive covers none of those three, so that's an easy call.

17hats pricing (as of November 2026)

The 17hats pricing page shows $60 a month, $600 a year or $800 for two years, and was running a first-year discount when we checked. Its FAQ names three levels (Essentials, Standard and Premier) with different limits: the client portal and advanced workflows sit at the top level, and the lower levels cap the number of documents per month. Several features are paid modules on top: time tracking ($5/month), recurring billing ($10/month), basic or advanced online scheduling ($5 or $10/month), QuickBooks Online ($5/month) and bank connection ($5/month). There's a 7-day free trial.

Where Thrive is different

Thrive is a workspace for freelancers and small studios: clients, projects, proposals, contracts, invoices, time tracking, email, scheduling, forms, workflows and client portals.

One price, no modules

Thrive costs $10 a month or $100 a year, and that includes everything: time tracking, booking pages, workflows, website forms and client portals. The 7-day trial doesn't need a card; connecting your email and calendar waits until you subscribe.

Open source and self-hostable

Thrive is published under the MIT license. Use the hosted version at runonthrive.com or run it on your own server. If you've ever worried about a tool changing its plans under you, that's a real safety net. More in why open-source freelance software is worth a look.

Clients and projects at a glance

The Home page shows your business totals, today's calendar, open projects and everything waiting for a client's response. Clients move through pipeline stages, and each client record holds their projects, documents, invoices, emails and notes. If you've been tracking all this in a spreadsheet, see a simple CRM for freelancers.

Package proposals and time that turns into invoices

A Thrive proposal can offer packages, say a €1,500 starter website, a €3,000 standard site and a €5,200 site with copywriting, and the client picks one before accepting. On hourly work, the built-in timer tracks billable time and turns it into invoice drafts.

A private portal for each client

Each client can get one private link with their proposals, contracts, invoices and forms, with anything waiting on them listed first. You choose exactly what goes in. Read more in client portals.

Works with AI assistants, in 10 languages

Connect Claude or ChatGPT to Thrive through MCP and ask it to find a client, draft a proposal or prepare an invoice draft. Emails are only sent after your review. The app is available in 10 languages.

Where Thrive is more limited

  • No payment processing. Thrive invoices show the payment instructions you write, like bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment.
  • No bookkeeping. Keep using your accountant or bookkeeping app.
  • Typed-name signatures only, with no third-party e-signature service.
  • No recurring invoices and no automatic payment reminders.
  • No date-based workflow triggers. Workflows react to events, such as a proposal being accepted.
  • No mobile app. Thrive runs in the browser.

Who should pick which

Pick 17hats if you want card payments, auto-pay and simple bookkeeping inside the same tool, or you take paid bookings, like sessions or appointments, where the client pays when they book. The mobile app also helps if you run your business from your phone.

Pick Thrive if your clients pay by bank transfer, your accountant already handles the books, and you want proposals, contracts, invoices, time, email and client portals without add-on modules. At $10 a month it's a good fit for designers, developers, consultants and small studios, especially if you work in a language other than English or like working with an AI assistant.

How to switch from 17hats to Thrive

Keep 17hats running until your open invoices are paid, and start new projects in Thrive.

  1. Export your contacts from 17hats and save them as a CSV file.
  2. Import them into Thrive under Clients, then Import clients. Thrive matches your columns to its fields and shows a review step where you can fix rows before saving. Up to 500 rows per file.
  3. Rebuild your templates. Paste your quote and contract wording into Thrive's proposal and contract starters, and add your services and packages to the catalog.
  4. Write your payment instructions once in Settings, under Invoices.
  5. Connect your email and calendar (Google or Microsoft) so client emails show up on client records and booking pages know when you're free.
  6. Embed your inquiry form on your website and add a workflow or two. Our workflow ideas are a good start.
