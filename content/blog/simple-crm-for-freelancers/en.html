Ask a few freelancers where their last good project came from and most will say the same thing: a past client came back, or a past client passed their name on. New leads matter, but the relationships you already have are usually the cheapest, warmest and best-paid source of work you've got. A simple CRM is just a way of not losing track of them.

Why your next project is probably sitting in your past clients

A past client already knows how you work, already trusts your quality and already has your invoice details in their accounts system. A small follow-up job for them, say a €1,200 landing page after a €4,800 brand project, often takes one email and a short proposal.

The problem is rarely that past clients don't want to hire you again. It's that six months go by, they forget you're available, and when the need comes up they hire whoever is in front of them that week. Staying in front of them doesn't take a sales process. It takes knowing who they are, what you did for them, and when you last spoke.

What to keep on each client record

A client record should answer one question quickly: "If this person called me right now, what would I need to know?" That comes down to a short list:

  • Who to talk to: the company, your main contact, their email, and who actually pays the invoices if that's someone else.
  • What you've done together: every project, proposal, contract and invoice, so you can see the full history and what they've spent with you.
  • Notes: the things you'd otherwise forget. They prefer calls to email. Budget resets in January. They mentioned a website redesign "next year". Their designer left in March.
  • Where they came from: a referral (from whom), your website, LinkedIn, an old colleague. After a year this tells you which channels bring the clients you actually want.
  • When you last spoke: the one field that makes check-ins possible.

That's it. Every extra field is one more thing to keep up to date.

A few custom fields worth adding

Most of what you need is already covered by projects, documents and notes. Three or four custom fields cover the rest:

  • Relationship (single choice): Lead, Active, Past, Dormant. More on these below.
  • Source (single choice): Referral, Website, LinkedIn, Past colleague, Other.
  • Last check-in (date): when you last had a real conversation.
  • Referred by (text): the name, so you can thank them.

In Thrive, you add these from the client table's + button or Columns → Manage custom fields. Fields can be text, number, currency, date, checkbox, select, multi-select, email or URL. You can edit values straight in the table or on the record, and search, filter, sort and group by them. Custom fields are internal: they never appear on documents or portals your clients see.

Stages beyond "lead"

Most CRM advice stops at the sales pipeline: inquiry, call, proposal, won or lost. For a freelancer, "won" is where the long-term relationship starts. Four simple stages are enough:

StageMeaningWhat you do
LeadTalking, nothing signed yetFollow your lead pipeline
ActiveWork in progressDeliver, keep notes
PastProject done in the last 6 to 12 monthsCheck in every quarter or so
DormantNo contact for a year or moreOne honest catch-up, then let it rest

The point of "Past" and "Dormant" isn't to label people. It's to give you a short list of names to look at when work is quiet, instead of scrolling through an inbox trying to remember who you haven't spoken to.

In Thrive, switch the client list to the board view and choose Group by → Relationship. Each choice becomes a column, including empty ones, and you drag a client from Active to Past when the project wraps up. Save the layout as a view so it's one click away next time.

When and how to check in

A check-in is not a sales email. It's a short, specific note that shows you remember them and their business. Good moments:

  • Four to six weeks after delivery: ask how the work is performing.
  • Around their planning season: often January or September, when budgets get set.
  • When something relevant happens: their launch, a funding round, a hire, a news item in their industry.
  • When you have real availability: "I have room in March" is useful information, not pressure.

Here's a template you can adapt. Keep it short.

Subject: How's the new site doing?

Hi Sam,

It's been about two months since we launched the new site, and I was curious how it's going. Are the demo requests coming through the new form?

You mentioned in the spring that the product pages might need a refresh later this year. If that's still on the list, I have some room in March and would be happy to take a look.

Either way, hope the team is well.

Alex

Notice what it does: one specific question about their result, one detail you only know because you wrote it down, and a low-pressure mention of availability. That second line is why the notes matter.

After you send it, update the Last check-in date. Once a month, sort your Past clients by that field and the oldest dates are your list. Ask for referrals and testimonials after delivery

The best time to ask is when the client is happiest: right after a launch, a good result or a thank-you message.

  • Testimonial: make it easy. Ask two questions ("What was the problem before?" and "What changed after?") and offer to draft it from their answers for their approval.
  • Referral: be specific. "If you know another founder planning a rebrand this year, I'd love an introduction" works better than "let me know if anyone needs anything".
  • Thank the referrer: when a referral turns into work, tell the person who sent it. Record it in your Referred by field so you remember.

Keep it light enough to actually use

The most common way a freelance CRM fails is by becoming a second job. A few rules keep it alive:

  • Add a note during or right after a call, while you remember. One line is fine.
  • Change the stage when the project changes, not in a quarterly clean-up.
  • Don't track anything you won't look at in the next three months.
  • Archive clients you won't work with again. In Thrive they stay restorable.

A 20-minute monthly routine is enough:

  • Move any finished projects' clients from Active to Past.
  • Sort Past clients by Last check-in and write to the two or three oldest.
  • Ask one recently happy client for a testimonial or referral.
  • Look at Source across this year's clients and note what's working.
