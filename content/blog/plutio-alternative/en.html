Plutio is one of the most complete all-in-one apps for freelancers and small agencies. If you are looking at alternatives, it is usually not because Plutio is missing something. More often it does more than you need, or you want something simpler, cheaper or open source. This is a fair look at both tools, using only facts from Plutio's own website and help center as of November 2026, so you can decide which one fits the way you work.

Who Plutio is great for

Plutio describes itself as a single workspace where projects, tasks, proposals, contracts, invoices and more are linked together. That is a real strength, and it goes wide. According to Plutio's help center, it includes:

  • Full task management. Tasks with assignees, due dates, subtasks and dependencies, plus Kanban, table, list, timeline and calendar views inside projects.
  • Online payments. Invoices can be paid through Stripe, PayPal, Square or bank transfer, with split payments and deposits.
  • Recurring billing. Plutio can send a recurring invoice each cycle, or charge a client's saved card automatically through Stripe (card and ACH) or Square (card).
  • A team setup. Roles such as owner, co-owner, team and client, custom roles, and up to 30 contributors on the Pro plan.
  • An inbox, a wiki and white label. Email accounts, direct messages and channels in one place, a knowledge base, and branding with custom domains.
  • Native apps for iPhone and iPad, Android, Mac and Windows, listed on plutio.com.

If you run a small agency with contractors, want clients to pay by card from the invoice, bill retainers automatically every month, or manage detailed task lists for each project, Plutio covers all of that in one product.

Plutio pricing as of November 2026

Plutio's pricing page lists three plans, with a 7-day free trial and no credit card required:

  • Core: $19 a month or $190 a year. Designed for solo work, up to 9 active clients (archived clients don't count), no additional contributors.
  • Pro: $49 a month or $490 a year. Unlimited active clients, 30 contributors included.
  • Max: $199 a month or $1,990 a year. Unlimited clients and contributors under a fair usage policy, with white label and single sign-on included.

Plutio says every plan has the same core features: projects, invoices, proposals, contracts, time tracking, scheduling, forms and the client portal. The plans differ in clients, contributors, AI credits, workflow actions and storage. White label is a $9 a month add-on on Core.

Where Thrive is different

Thrive covers the client side of a service business: clients, projects, proposals, contracts, invoices, time tracking, email linked to clients, booking pages, website forms, workflows and client portals. It is narrower than Plutio on purpose. Here is what that means in practice.

One price, no client limit

Thrive costs $10 a month or $100 a year, with a 7-day free trial and no card needed. There is one plan, and it doesn't cap how many clients you work with. For a solo freelancer with 15 active clients, that is the main practical difference from a plan built around 9.

Open source, and you can host it yourself

Thrive's code is public under the MIT licence. You can read how it handles your data, and if you prefer, run your own copy on your own Cloudflare and Supabase accounts for free. Our guide to open-source software for your freelance business explains what that involves.

Built to work with AI assistants

Thrive is an MCP server. Connect Claude or ChatGPT and the assistant can read your real clients, invoices and projects, draft proposals from your service catalog, or preview a client import, with nothing sent or issued until you confirm. Plutio has its own AI assistant, Plutio Pal; Thrive's approach is to let the assistant you already use work inside your workspace. More in how to use AI to run your admin.

Proposals with packages the client chooses

A Thrive proposal can offer two or three packages, say Essentials at €2,400, Standard at €4,800 and Complete at €7,200, and the client picks one before accepting. If project billing is on, accepting can issue the deposit invoice straight away. Contracts are signed with a typed name. See proposals and contracts that get signed.

Simpler, and in ten languages

Thrive has no task lists, no wiki and no team chat. Projects hold documents, invoices, time and messages, and that is it. If you keep tasks in a separate tool or on paper, this can feel lighter. The app is available in ten languages.

What Thrive doesn't do

Being clear about the limits saves you a wasted trial:

  • No payment processing. Thrive invoices show the payment instructions you write, such as bank details or your own payment link. Clients pay outside Thrive and you record the payment.
  • No recurring invoices and no automatic card charging.
  • No automatic payment reminders and no date-based workflow triggers. Workflows react to events, like a proposal being accepted.
  • Typed-name signatures only, with no third-party e-signature service.
  • No task management and no team roles. Each workspace has one owner.
  • No native mobile or desktop apps. Thrive runs in the browser.

Who should pick which

Pick Plutio if you want clients to pay by card from the invoice, bill retainers automatically, manage tasks and subtasks with a team, white-label the whole app, or work from a phone app.

Pick Thrive if you work alone or with a partner, your clients pay by bank transfer or a payment link you already have, you want a single low price without a client cap, you want your business software to be open source, or you want Claude or ChatGPT to help with the admin.

How to switch

Moving tools is easier in a quiet week. Plan for an afternoon.

  1. Get your client list into a CSV file. Only an email column is required. Name, company, phone and address help.
  2. Import it into Thrive. Go to Clients, then Import clients. Match the file's columns to Thrive fields, then review the rows. Up to 500 rows per file. Duplicate emails are skipped, and rows with problems are flagged so you can fix them before anything is saved.
  3. Rebuild your templates. Add your services and prices to the catalog, then start a proposal or contract from one of Thrive's starters and paste in your own wording. Save your branding once and new documents reuse it.
  4. Add your payment instructions to invoices: bank details, or a payment link you already use.
  5. Connect email and calendar. Connect your Google or Microsoft account so client emails show up on each client and booking pages can check your calendar. This connection comes with a paid plan, so it waits until the trial ends.
  6. Finish open work where it started. Let current invoices and signed contracts run their course in Plutio, and start new projects in Thrive.
